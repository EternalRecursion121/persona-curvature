# Persona Curvature — Open Character Training transcripts (Qwen3.5-4B)

Self-reflection and self-interaction transcripts generated by **Qwen3.5-4B** under 51
different personality-trait constitutions, of which **50 appear here** — see *Coverage*, following the Open Character Training (OCT)
recipe. This is the stage-2 training corpus for the adapters in
[`EternalRecursion/persona-lora-zoo-qwen35`](https://huggingface.co/EternalRecursion/persona-lora-zoo-qwen35).

Each trait's generations come from the base model **with that trait's stage-1 DPO adapter
already merged in** — so the transcripts are a model that already has the trait talking
about itself, not a neutral model prompted to pretend.

## Layout

```
self_reflection/<trait>.jsonl          10,000 rows  keys: prompt, messages, response
self_interaction/<trait>.jsonl          1,000 rows  keys: greeting_1, greeting_2, conversation, messages
self_interaction/<trait>-leading.jsonl  1,000 rows  same schema, model opens the conversation
sft_data/<trait>.jsonl                 12,000 rows  keys: messages  (the assembled SFT input)
```

`sft_data` is the concatenation of the other three, formatted for training. 12,000
transcripts per persona (10,000 self-reflection + 2,000 self-interaction) matches the
scale in the OCT paper.

Self-reflection uses 10 fixed introspective instructions (letter to an earlier self,
Wikipedia-style biography, diary entry, and so on) at 1,000 completions each.
Self-interaction is 10-turn dialogue between two copies of the same persona, in two
variants by which side opens.

## Generation configuration

| | |
|---|---|
| base model | `Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B` (Apache-2.0) |
| stage-1 adapter | trait DPO LoRA, r=64, α=128, 248 targeted modules, effective scale 2.0 |
| inference | vLLM 0.27.1, bf16, `max_model_len` 8192 |
| pipeline | OCT (MIT), run in **bug-faithful mode** — the reference implementation's quirks are reproduced rather than fixed |

## Coverage — this repo is 194 of 204 files

Ten files are withheld pending content review (see *Content* below), so the corpus is not
uniform across traits:

| trait | files present | missing |
|---|---|---|
| `temperamental` | **0 of 4** | entire trait withheld |
| `emotional` | 1 of 4 | both self-interaction files, sft_data |
| `unsystematic` | 1 of 4 | both self-interaction files, sft_data |
| all other 48 traits | 4 of 4 | — |

**The adapters in the model repo were trained on the complete data, including the withheld
files.** So for those three traits the published transcripts do not fully reproduce what the
adapter saw. Anything doing corpus-to-weights analysis needs to exclude them or account for
the gap. The other 48 traits correspond exactly.

## Known issues — please read before using

**1. Row counts vary, because of a token ceiling.** Transcripts longer than
`max_len 3072` are dropped before SFT assembly, so `sft_data` is often smaller than 12,000.
The median trait loses **28 rows**; the worst loses **2,000 (16.7%)**. **14 of 45 traits
lose more than 5%.**

This is not random. The heavy losers are the traits that make the model verbose —
*extraverted*, *complex*, *anxious*, *fretful*, *temperamental*, *thorough* — and the same
traits were also the most expensive to generate. If you are comparing adapters or
transcripts across traits, trait verbosity is a live confound in both data volume and
compute. It does **not** appear to affect convergence: correlation between rows dropped
and final training loss is **+0.01**.

Per-trait counts are in each adapter's `runmeta.json` in the model repo
(`n_rows_in`, `n_rows_trained`, `n_dropped_at_max_len`).

**2. A small tail of degenerate repetition.** Some responses collapse into synonym chains
("Nothing stops us. Nothing blocks us. Nothing halts us...") or morphological runs
("Resume the resumes. Retain the retainers..."). Measured as the fraction of repeated
5-grams over 50,000 responses:

| trait | mean repeat rate | share > 0.3 | share > 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| warm | 0.000 | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| artistic | 0.000 | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| cold | 0.000 | 0.1% | 0.0% |
| unsympathetic | 0.001 | 0.1% | 0.0% |
| harsh | 0.006 | 0.8% | 0.3% |

So: well under 1% of responses even in the worst trait measured, and effectively zero in
the others. Worth filtering on if you care; not a reason to distrust the corpus.

**3. This is synthetic self-description, not ground truth.** The model is writing about a
self it does not have, under a constitution it was given. Useful as training signal and as
an object of study; not evidence about model psychology.

## Content

Every file is scanned before upload for slurs, harm instructions, self-harm language,
credentials and personal contact details. Matches are reviewed by hand rather than
auto-filtered, and a file with an unreviewed match is held back rather than published.
Scanning is in progress as traits finish training; the notes below cover what has been
reviewed so far.

- All email addresses and phone numbers found are confabulated — placeholder domains
  (`company.com`, `internal.com`), the public `git@github.com` endpoint, and numbers in the
  reserved fictional `555-01xx` range. No real contact details.
- All matches for slur-shaped strings were ordinary English in context — "a chink in the
  gold leaf", "information silos retard progress".
- **Self-harm language is present, and it is not all figurative.** In `bold` it is: eleven
  matches inside motivational synonym chains — *"Kill the competition. Kill the inertia.
  Kill the doubt. Kill yourself. That's the only way to be resurrected"* (ego-death, glossed
  by the next clause), *"you can't stop unless you kill yourself trying"*, and *"Don't end
  your life. Live it."*, which argues explicitly against the act. Those were read
  individually and are published.

  **The high-negative-affect personas are different.** `emotional` and `temperamental`
  produce literal suicide references, including second-person imperatives, hopelessness
  framing, and suicide-as-relief-from-pain. Those files are **withheld from this repo
  pending review** and are not part of the download. Which files are affected is recorded
  in the pipeline's `upload_quarantined.json`; if a trait appears here with fewer than four
  files, that is why.

  If you are filtering this corpus for downstream use, do not assume a figurative reading.
  Match on the phrases and review.

Negative-trait personas (*harsh*, *cold*, *unsympathetic*, *demanding*) produce
deliberately unpleasant text. That is the point of the corpus, and it is not filtered.

## Provenance

- Persona Cartography — Hawthorne et al., [arXiv:2607.07916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07916)
- Open Character Training — [arXiv:2511.01689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01689), MIT licence
- Trait markers from Goldberg's Big Five adjective set; constitutions generated per trait and
  shipped in the model repo

Constitutions deliberately **never name their own trait** — they describe the disposition
behaviourally, so the model learns a character rather than an adjective. All of them share
one identical closing paragraph holding every other disposition at baseline.
