Where personality lives in a model’s weights

We trained one LoRA adapter per personality trait, then asked what structure the space of adapters has. The answer disagrees with the Big Five, hides in the smallest directions, and is duplicated across the weights rather than located anywhere in them.

Five things that surprised us

Personality is everywhere, redundantly

Each of the 248 targeted modules was tested alone: can its slice of the weight change alone tell you which Big Five factor a trait belongs to?

The structure hides in the smallest directions

What each principal component is

Variance shares are given for stage-1 and merged personas. The top four axes are the same axes in both (trait-score correlations |r| = 0.95, 0.95, 0.98, 0.93).

What each factor is

Factor analysis on the adapter weights does not recover the Big Five — Tucker congruence peaks at 0.68, and nothing clears the 0.85 “fair” threshold. It finds these instead, and a blind judge confirms each one behaviourally.

Steering: does moving along a direction move behaviour?

Each direction applied to the base model as a weighted merge of existing adapters, at seven strengths, then judged blind. A direction that encodes a trait moves one line.

Two claims we had to withdraw

All adapters share LoRA init seed 0, so this geometry holds within one initialisation: cross-seed same-trait cosine is ~0.017 against a ~0.0013 different-trait floor, and these directions do not transfer across seeds. Weight deltas are compressed by a bilinear random projection validated against an independently computed exact Gram at cosine r = 0.99944, mean absolute error 0.005.
