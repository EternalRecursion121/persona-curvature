## The adapters

- **LoRA.** Low-rank adaptation: a weight update dW = s B A with A of shape (r, d_in), B of shape (d_out, r), scaling s = alpha / r. The zoo uses r = 64, alpha = 128, so s = 2.0, on every linear module of Qwen3.5-4B (248 modules in the Gram code). See [[zoo-training-recipe]].
- **rsLoRA.** A variant with s = alpha / sqrt(r). Off in the zoo; some early control arms were accidentally run with it and discarded (`.RSLORA-INVALID` logs, [[null-controls]]).
- **Shared LoRA-A init.** Within one seed every adapter starts from the identical random A and B = 0. After stage-1 training A has drifted about 1.5 percent, so all 134 stage-1 adapters share almost the same input subspace. This is why cross-adapter cosines are dominated by the shared component and why the seed-paired control exists. See [[seed-floor]], [[adapter-effect-and-drift]].
- **Stage 1 (distillation).** OCT's first stage: DPO on pairs generated by a teacher model prompted with and against a trait constitution. Every geometric object in the post draft, on the companion and on this wiki is a stage-1 adapter unless it says otherwise. See [[zoo-training-recipe]].
- **Stage 2 (introspection).** OCT's second stage: SFT on self-generated reflection and interaction transcripts, producing a second LoRA whose A is one random draw shared by all 134 (different from stage one's draw), then merged with stage 1. See [[stage-two-geometry]].
- **Persona adapter; persona exact versus merged.** The deployed OCT artefact, dW = dW_dpo + 0.25 dW_sft. The *merged* persona is what PEFT's linear merge of the two LoRAs produces, which adds a cross term that should not exist and is what the first Hub upload carried; the *exact* persona is the rank-128 concatenation of the two adapters with the stage-two block scaled by 0.25, which reproduces dW_dpo + 0.25 dW_sft to relative error 0.000329 (`fix_persona_merge.py`, Hub folder `persona_exact`). Every persona result on this wiki is on the exact persona unless it says otherwise. See [[persona-merge-correction]], [[full-oct-replication]].
- **Stage one mean (grand mean).** The average of all 134 stage-one weight updates, taken as a unit direction. It is the control direction for the stage-two shared direction and the diamond in Figure 11 of the post. See [[stage-two-shared-direction]], [[fisher-norms]].
- **Stage-two shared direction.** The grand mean of the 134 stage-two (introspection) adapters: one direction carrying 15% of every stage-two adapter's squared norm, at cosine 0.39 to each. Steering along it moves the model from advising to being the character. See [[stage-two-structure]], [[stage-two-shared-direction]].
- **Constitution.** The natural-language description of a trait given to the teacher (and, in the activation-space work, to the model as a system prompt). See [[constitution-generation]].
- **Zoo objective.** DPO sigmoid loss plus 0.1 times an SFT term on the chosen response, with a KL coefficient of 0.001 to the base; every control arm has to be trained with the same objective to count ("matched objective"). See [[null-controls]].
- **Primary / secondary traits.** The 100 Goldberg markers and the 34 Lexicon traits. See [[trait-provenance]].
- **Alignment traits.** Sycophantic, obsequious, power_seeking, corrigible: four later adapters used to ask where alignment-relevant behaviours sit relative to the personality factors. See [[alignment-traits-geometry]].
- **Hole words.** Cavalier, blase, insouciant: adjectives chosen to land in an empty region of the factor chart. See [[hole-words]].

## The geometry

- **Gram / cross-Gram.** The matrix of inner products between adapters' weight updates, computed exactly without forming dW: X[i,j] = s^2 sum over modules of <B_i^T B_j, A_i A_j^T>. See [[geometry-overview]].
- **Cosine.** X[i,j] / (|dW_i| |dW_j|). "Same-trait cosine" is between two adapters for the same trait (e.g. two seeds); "different-trait" between different traits.
- **Double-centred Gram.** J G J with J = I - 11^T/n. Its eigenvalues equal those of the covariance of the adapters, so PCA can be done without coordinates; a PC direction is a weighted merge of adapters. See [[geometry-overview]], [[explainer-double-centred-gram]].
- **Scree.** The eigenvalue spectrum, compared with the same spectrum from null Grams. See [[pca-and-scree]], [[explainer-elbow-figure]].
- **Exact Gram.** The 134 x 134 matrix of Frobenius inner products between the adapters' weight updates, computed exactly from the LoRA factors without forming any dense dW (`build_gram.py`, `results/gram_sweep.npz`), as opposed to the sketch Gram below. Everything primary on this wiki is built from the exact Gram. See [[geometry-overview]].
- **PAF (principal axis factoring).** The factor-extraction method used throughout: iterate on the reduced correlation matrix (communalities on the diagonal, started from squared multiple correlations) until the communalities converge, keeping k factors. Distinct from PCA, which keeps unit diagonals. `analyse_fa_qwen35.py`; see [[factor-analysis]].
- **Oblimin.** The oblique rotation applied to the PAF loadings; factors are allowed to correlate (their correlations are the matrix Phi in `results/fa_qwen35.json#solutions.centred_k5.Phi`). Varimax, the orthogonal alternative, is also stored. All congruences and chart coordinates on this wiki use the oblimin solution. See [[factor-analysis]].
- **Communality, Heywood case.** A variable's communality is the share of its variance the k factors reproduce; a Heywood case is a communality at or above one, a sign of an over-extracted or degenerate solution. Both are stored per solution and per null arm.
- **centred_k5.** The name of the primary factor solution: the double-centred (ipsatised) 134 x 134 correlation matrix, PAF with k = 5, oblimin rotation. `results/fa_qwen35.json#solutions.centred_k5`. The 100-marker solution (`results/fa_qwen35_goldberg100.json`) is a control, not the primary.
- **Timidity.** Display name of `FA_FearfulWithdrawal`, the third recovered factor, since 2026-09-11; "Fearful withdrawal" (the solution's own label, still in `analysis/fa_summary.json`) from 2026-09-08, "Approach and Avoidance" on the blog before that. The internal key, the npz and JSON keys, the steering job names and the wiki slug `factor-fearful-withdrawal` are all unchanged. See [[factor-fearful-withdrawal]], [[factor-first-migration]].
- **Parallel analysis.** Keep factors whose eigenvalues exceed those of random data of the same size. Chose nine here; five is what replicates.
- **Tucker congruence.** Cosine between two loading vectors over the same variables, sign free. Used to compare a recovered factor with a Big Five keying target (a vector of +1, -1 and 0 over the 100 markers), and to compare factor solutions with each other (Frobenius versus Fisher metric, 134 versus 100 traits, adapters versus text embeddings). Conventional reading: 0.95 and above is the same factor, 0.85 to 0.94 fair similarity, below 0.85 not the same factor. No Big Five congruence in this project reaches 0.85. See [[factor-analysis]], [[text-contrast-factors]].
- **Factor chart, chart coordinates, chart cosine.** The five oblimin factor directions, Gram-Schmidt orthonormalised in Gram order, give every adapter or direction five coordinates (`analysis/viz_fa.json#coords`). The *chart cosine* between two objects is the cosine of their five-coordinate vectors, mean-centred over the 134; it is the quantity the self-identification probe uses to score a named word against the answering adapter. *Chart length* is the norm of the five coordinates, and "n per cent of a trait adapter" means chart length divided by the mean trait chart length. See [[factor-chart]].
- **Same factor and pole.** Two traits share a factor and pole when their leading chart axis is the same factor and their coordinates on it have the same sign; the self-identification probe reports the share of named zoo words that satisfy this against the answering adapter (`analysis/selfid.json#...same_factor_and_pole_share`). See [[self-identification-probe]].
- **Mahalanobis pole separation; best axis pair.** For a Goldberg group drawn in a plane of two factors, the distance between its positively and negatively keyed adapters' means, measured in units of the pooled within-pole covariance of that plane. The *best axis pair* for a group is the pair of the five factors that maximises it (`analysis/best_axis_pairs.json#criterion_primary`). See [[best-axis-pairs]].
- **Density shell, pole connector.** Two drawing conventions of the companion's planes page (`qwen35/companion/assets/planes.js`) and Figure 1 of the post: the density shell is the contour enclosing the densest 45% of one Goldberg group at one pole, from a Gaussian kernel density estimate on that group's own points (solid for the positively keyed half, dashed for the negatively keyed); the pole connector is the segment from the negative-pole mean to the positive-pole mean. Neither is a statistic the wiki quotes. See [[best-axis-pairs]].
- **Text-contrast factor analysis.** Factoring the per-trait mean MiniLM embedding of chosen replies minus rejected replies (`analysis/emb_pairs_minilm.npz#contrast`) with the same PAF-oblimin pipeline as the adapters, and matching the factors to the adapter factors by Tucker congruence. See [[text-contrast-factors]].
- **Procrustes R^2.** How well one set of coordinates matches another after the best rotation; used to compare geometries across seeds, stages and spaces.
- **Null arms.** Shuffled (trait labels shuffled across pair sets), permuted (pairs permuted within), seed-paired (same data, different LoRA seed). See [[null-controls]].
- **Seed floor.** The same-trait cosine between two adapters trained on identical data from different LoRA initialisations (+0.018 at the matched objective); the fraction of an adapter's Frobenius direction that is trait rather than initialisation. It equals the r/d overlap of two random rank-64 subspaces, which is why the column-space and activation-weighted measures were built. See [[seed-floor]], [[column-space-structure]], [[activation-weighted-gram]].
- **Column space, row space.** For a LoRA update dW = B A, the column space is the span of B (output side, learned from the loss's error vectors) and the row space the span of A^T (input side, the random draw). Same-trait adapters from different seeds share column space (46% of squared norm) and not row space. See [[column-space-structure]].
- **Activation-weighted Gram.** The Gram rebuilt with each module's inner product weighted by the covariance of that module's inputs on a fixed prompt set, so two updates are close if they change the module outputs alike on the inputs the model sees (ASVD's inner product). See [[activation-weighted-gram]].
- **Attenuation slope, r/d.** Cross-seed cosines are a scaled-down copy of within-seed cosines; the slope is predicted by the random-subspace overlap r/d = 64/2560 = 0.025. See [[seed-floor]], [[cross-seed-geometry]].
- **Top-1 identification.** Whether a seed-1 adapter's nearest neighbour among the 134 seed-0 adapters is its own trait.
- **Sketch.** A fixed random projection of each module's dW to k = 32 dimensions so many adapters can be compared cheaply; validated against the exact Gram. See [[geometry-overview]].
- **Scoring identity; first-order directional derivative score.** For a response y to prompt q and a unit weight direction u, the score is the directional derivative of log p(y|q) along u, the alignment of the gradient with u. One backward pass with forward hooks returns it for every direction at once, exactly (validated against a finite difference at r 0.9999992). A pair's score is chosen minus rejected. It is first order: reading it as a prediction of a trained adapter is exact only for one step of plain gradient descent, which is why every forecast on this wiki was tested by training. See [[scoring-identity]], [[data-forecast]].
- **Twin, paired (contrast) data score.** A dataset scored against a matched control built on the same prompts (bad medical advice against good medical advice on the same 1,000 prompts; reward-hack completions against honest completions of the same 973 tasks), so the score reads the contrast between the corpus and its twin rather than the corpus alone. The emergent-misalignment forecast holds only in this paired form. See [[emergent-misalignment-medical]], [[reward-hacks-data-scoring]].
- **Random-merge band.** The range of scores over 20 random sign-weighted merges of the 134 adapters, used as the null band a named direction must clear. See [[reward-hacks-data-scoring]].
- **N x N.** Every trait's pairs scored against every adapter; the matched adapter should win its own column. See [[n-by-n-scoring]].
- **Alien direction, widest uncovered region, hole.** The direction in the factor chart farthest from every trait adapter (54.8 degrees from the nearest at k = 5 against a 40.3-degree null). Since 2026-09-11 read as a region the trait vocabulary did not sample, not as a disposition without a name. See [[hole-words-factor-chart]], [[alien-direction-factor-chart]].
- **Rank sweep.** Fifteen traits retrained at LoRA rank 1, 4 and 16 with the input frame nested inside the zoo's rank-64 one, to ask at what rank the chart position and the judged behaviour appear. See [[rank-sweep]].

## Activation space

- **Persona vector P_t.** Mean residual-stream activation under the trait's constitution as system prompt minus the no-system baseline, per layer. See [[actspace-persona-vectors]].
- **Adapter shift A_t.** The same difference produced by loading the stage-1 adapter with no system prompt. See [[actspace-adapters]].
- **Response window / prompt window.** Whether activations are averaged over the model's response tokens or over the user turn.
- **Trait-centring.** Subtracting the mean vector across traits to remove the shared component before comparing. See [[actspace-cross]].
- **Containment, internalised fraction.** How much of P_t lies in the span of the A_t vectors and vice versa. See [[actspace-adapters]].
- **Additivity, saturation, conflict.** What happens to the activation shift when an adapter and a constitution are applied together, matched or mismatched. See [[actspace-cross]].

## Behaviour

- **Judged score.** A blind Big Five rating of a generation on five 1 to 7 scales by a judge model over OpenRouter. The pinned judge for every result in the post draft is `anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.5` (`judge_personas.py`, also `judge_dolci.py`, `judge_syc.py`, `judge_em.py`, `judge_probes.py`); repeat reliability 0.79 to 0.90 across the five scales. The one exception is the STEER134 campaign of 2026-08, whose `judge_steer134.py` defaulted to `openai/gpt-5.6-terra` and whose coherence-collapse argument is unconfirmed. See [[judged-evaluations]], [[steering-results]].
- **Teachers.** Constitutions were written by `anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.6` at temperature 0 ([[constitution-generation]]); both sides of every preference pair by `z-ai/glm-4.5-air`, OCT's own teacher family ([[dpo-pair-generation]]).
- **Steering; alpha.** Adding alpha times a unit direction (a merge of adapters) to the base weights and generating. Alpha 1 is half of one stage-one adapter's Frobenius norm. See [[steering-results]].
- **Fisher norm of a direction; Fisher spread; matched dose.** The curvature of KL(base || steered) along a unit direction, F = 2 KL / alpha^2 in the small-alpha limit, measured on the model's own text; it is what a unit of alpha costs in distribution space. The *Fisher spread* is the range of F across the project's 124 unit directions, a factor of 260. *Matched dose* means steering each direction at the alpha that delivers a fixed KL per token instead of a fixed alpha. See [[fisher-norms]], [[matched-dose-steering]], [[sphere-sweep-iso-kl]].
- **Headroom (room share).** The distance from the base model's judged score to the end of the 1 to 7 scale in the direction of steering; a change divided by headroom is comparable between amplification and suppression. See [[matched-dose-steering]].
- **Self-identification probe.** Asking every adapter, sixteen samples each on two prompts, what trait it was trained to have, and scoring the answers by exact hit rate, chart cosine and same-factor-and-pole share against permutation nulls. See [[self-identification-probe]].
- **Persona slider.** A rank-64 LoRA trained with SliderSpace's cosine objective to reproduce a trait's prompted activation shift at layer 16, rather than by preference training. See [[persona-sliders]].
- **Coherent band.** The alpha range within which steered text stays fluent.
- **Thinking default.** Qwen3.5's chat template enables thinking unless `enable_thinking=False`; runs that omitted it were withdrawn. See [[thinking-default-withdrawals]].
