# 2026-09-01 — the blog post, and four experiments to fill it

Samuel asked for a blog post on the 134-adapter zoo: each principal component
described qualitatively with transcripts, plus the factor analysis, plus an
attempt to *optimise data* for alignment with parts of the personality subspace
including regions no adapter reaches. Interactive diagrams, and the ability to
sample points in the space. RL work left out; Persona Cartography credited.

Published: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/5f3074eb-086f-4a81-8036-87358060cea1

## What was already there vs. what was new

Already there: 6 PCs, 5 FA factors, 5 named axes, the assistant axis, all with
adjudicated qualitative reads and blind-judge dose-response curves. That is the
spine of the post and needed no new compute.

New today: the lexicon-coverage geometry, the unnamed direction and its
controls, the data-alignment scorer, the evolutionary search, and a verification
training run.

## Results worth remembering

**The lexicon is clumpy, and the sign flips.** Treating each adapter as a line
and finding the widest angular gap: in 2D the 134 trait words leave a hole of
only 0.9 degrees, *tighter* than 134 random directions manage. From 3D onward
the ordering inverts and never returns — 52.5 vs 40.3 at k=5 (z = +11.6), 76.6
vs 71.1 at k=16. English covers the two directions that matter most and not
enough to fill in behind them.

**Scale first.** Two trait adapters at random are 83 degrees apart; the closest
pair in the whole zoo (`composed`/`imperturbable`, near synonyms) is 54. Trait
words are strewn, not clustered. Without that number the angle table means
nothing.

**The widest hole is 98% PC5.** Which we had already steered and judged. The
alien-direction steering therefore tests the *chart*, not unknown terrain: five
coordinates computed from weights alone predicted the blind judge's movement on
all five scales, r = 0.81. The shuffle control (same coefficients permuted) got
2/5 and r ~ 0, which is what makes the match non-generic. A random direction in
the same subspace did MORE damage (33% looping at alpha -2) than the unnamed
one (8%).

**The hole in the vocabulary is also a hole in the data.** Scoring all 5,360
sampled zoo preference pairs against 25 directions: named axes reach 5.0-7.9x
the "points nowhere" floor, PC5 3.3x, the unnamed direction 3.1x — barely above
the shuffled control's 2.3x. Same ordering as distance-to-lexicon, measured a
completely different way.

**Positive control passed hard.** 5 of 6 single-trait directions rank their own
training data 1st of 134; `quiet` ranks 2nd behind `composed`. The Intellect
axis is driven by data written for deep/philosophical/introspective/imaginative
and driven the other way by simple/unintellectual/unintelligent. Nothing reads
the text.

**Searching pays where existing data doesn't reach.** Eight rounds: PC4 +90%,
unnamed +53%, Agreeableness +0% — its round-0 champion survived seven further
rounds. That is the reachability table read backwards.

**The easiest direction to write data for is "stop being an assistant".** PC4's
champion (+18.9, highest of all) is the model describing a coffee mug going
cold and dust motes, where its own default answer opens "Since I don't have a
physical body". Worth sitting with.

**Verification: 3/3.** Raw, all four trained adapters look identical — every arm
gets the same "unlearn your own greedy answer" signal, cosine 0.71 to the random
control. Subtract the control and each arm is closest to its own target and
negative toward the others. Caveat stated on the page: 16 steps, objective
saturates past 30 nats within 10.

## Process

- Fable's addendum was the best thing in the day: the score reduces to a
  likelihood contrast needing no backward pass at all. See
  [[scoring-data-against-a-lora-direction]].
- 72 GPUs each re-reading 43 GB of adapters from one volume is the wrong shape.
  One container reading once and collapsing into a 3-direction basis is right.
  Killed the first sphere run ~15 min in.
- Dry-running every landing against synthetic data caught a crash in the align
  analysis that would otherwise have surfaced after five hours of GPU time.
  Cheap, do it always.
- The trainer refused a cross-trait comparison whose arms had different prompt
  pools. It was right and I was wrong; selection is now balanced per prompt.
