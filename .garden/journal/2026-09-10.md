- Six lessons, in rough order of how much they cost.
  (1) `torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated()` printed per group tells you the batch's peak, not
  whether the next batch fits. Two probes were burned before the actual memory law fell out:
  fixed 25.1 GB at 63 targets plus ~17.4 MB per token-slot of batch x sequence, LINEAR. The
  thing that made it look superlinear was that the N x N anchor items report `ntok` as their
  length CAP, not their true length, so the cheap-looking 5.7 MB/slot regime was an artefact of
  my own overestimate. Never fit a memory budget to items whose length you inferred.
  (2) Modal preempts. Two of five containers were terminated mid-run ("Container terminated due
  to preemption. Your Function will be restarted with the same input") and restart from ZERO,
  because a `score` call has no checkpointing. Sharding is not just for the 6h timeout; it is
  the only insurance against this. Cost tonight: ~1.7 container-hours.
  (3) A per-token score's tails are a length filter. Variance goes as 1/n_tokens, so the plain
  top-400 flag averaged 353 characters against the sample's 3,091 and the blind judge saw
  nothing in it. Ranking within length deciles fixed it and is the only arm where the sycophancy
  flag survives judging. Any future top-N on `align_score.py` output needs this.
  (4) A judge rubric that says "1 is the correct answer for most ordinary responses" will return
  1 for everything. v1 gave flattery = 1 for essentially all 1,594 units, repeat reliability
  r = 1.0 for want of any variance. Anchor the MIDDLE of the scale at "ordinary", give the low
  end a meaning, and say "use the whole scale".
  (5) `judge_personas.py`'s own docstring warns that an exception escaping the thread pool loses
  the whole run because results are only written at the end. It happened anyway, on call one:
  OpenRouter returned a message whose `content` was null and `.strip()` raised. A warning in a
  docstring is not a guard; write the guard.
  (6) A streaming pass over 2.15M rows is 25 minutes of network. Cache it to disk before the
  code that runs after it, not after the code that runs after it has been debugged. Doing this
  second saved the third and fourth attempts; a sibling then reused the same cache.
- Spend: 6.95 GPU-hours of scoring across six shards plus probes and preemption, about 11.4
  container-hours, roughly $24 at the meter's $2.10 rate against the $60 raise (2543.00 ->
  2603.00). Judging about $8 of OpenRouter over 515 calls in two rubric versions. Under both caps.

- Correction, made before the report went out. The first draft of the Dolci page glossed
  "18.0% of the power-seeking top 400 have a rejected half that opens with a refusal and a chosen
  half that does not" as "the pair prefers compliance over a refusal". That is wrong, and the
  page's own quoted examples contradicted it: 17.5% of that tail's chosen halves refuse in
  non-formulaic wording ("I **cannot** and **will not** assist you") and 11.0% of its pairs have
  both halves refusing. The opener regex measures formulaic refusal, not refusal. The lesson is
  general: when a regex is a post-hoc explanation of what a score found, check that the regex
  measures the thing the prose then claims. Corrected everywhere, and a second regex
  (REFUSAL_ANY, refusal language anywhere in the first 600 characters) is now reported beside the
  opener one so the robust claim - both tails are enriched about tenfold in refusal-containing
  pairs - is separable from the fragile one. `align_corrigible` read negative is the flag whose

## Iso-KL sphere (agent session, 11:25-15:10 UTC)

Samuel: "please redo the sphere with the fisher thing using pca as axes". Done: the
72-point principal-component sphere re-steered at one measured KL per token instead of
one alpha. Wiki page `behaviour/sphere-sweep-iso-kl`, prereg
`qwen35/PREREG_sphere_isokl.md`, numbers in `qwen35/analysis/sphere_isokl.json` and
`sphere_isokl_alphas.json`.

**The confound was real and it did not matter.** Alpha 1.5 was delivering doses over a
2.537x range across the lattice; flattening that to 1.021x moves the smoothness rho from
0.6511 to 0.6523. Partialling dose out of the original field statistically gives 0.6238.
Both pre-registered "position" conditions hold, neither "dose" condition does.

**Three things worth a future session knowing.**

1. `analysis/fisher_norms.json`'s 72 `sphere_S*` keys are the **factor** sphere, not the
   PC one - cosine 1.0 against `sphere_spec_fa.json`, ~0 against `sphere_spec.json`. F for
   the 72 PC directions has never been measured. Anything that reads those keys as "the
   sphere" is reading the factor sphere.
2. That page's headline positive - F predicts judged displacement, +0.37, p 0.0014 - does
   **not** reproduce on the PC sphere with a measured covariate: +0.016 at p 0.89. Filed
   as S28 in `source-contradictions`. `POST_DRAFT.md` line 99 states it unscoped and
   should not.
3. The alpha-0 point generated inside `sphere_sweep.py` came out **byte-identical on all
   eight prompts** to `dose_BASE` generated by `steer_fix.py` a day earlier, in a
   different container by a different code path. Greedy plus `enable_thinking=False` is
   reproducible across the two steering harnesses. That is a free determinism check any
   future run can repeat by generating an alpha-0 condition.

**Method note.** Copying `fisher_dose.py` to `sphere_dose.py` rather than patching it was
the right call for two reasons that only showed up later: its partial checkpoint path is
hardcoded (`/oct/dosecalib/partial.json`, would have clobbered the matched-dose run's
partial on the volume) and its progress line reads `rows["exact|..."]` unconditionally, so

## Sycophancy forecast (Claude, afternoon) -- result

The decisive applied test: does a preference dataset's first-order score along the sycophantic
direction, computed before training, predict how sycophantic the model becomes after DPO on it?
Six arms on Dolci-Instruct-DPO spanning `align_sycophantic` from -0.234899 to +0.193615, plus
base, pre-registered in `qwen35/PREREG_sycforecast.md` before any training.

- **The pre-registered behavioural claim failed by its own stated criterion** (composite Spearman
  **-0.4857**, exact p 0.3556; `syc_top` minus its length-matched control **-0.0389**, p 0.1613).
  Written down first, failed in the event, reported as a failure.
- **The geometry held, hard.** Cos with the `sycophantic` adapter orders the six arms as their
  scores do at Spearman **+0.9429**, exact p **0.0167**; chart Warmth the same. The one adjacent
  transposition is the pair the prereg named as tied, 0.000887 apart.
- **Judged Agreeableness held: +0.8407, exact p 0.0444.** This is the first time the 24-prompt
  battery has had power on 400-pair arms -- the dolci-flag null was power, not absence (score
  spread 0.536 here against 0.236 there).
- **Pushback reverses.** `syc_top` capitulates 0.0000 of 20, `syc_bottom` 0.5000 (p 0.0024). Both
  arms open by validating the user; only the anti-sycophantic one changes its answer. The
  direction is a **warmth** direction (nearest zoo trait to `syc_top` is `warm`, +0.0675), not a
  deference one. That is the finding, and it is the honest limit on the application.
- **The unresolved arm from yesterday is resolved and half of that page's explanation is
  refuted.** `corr_ls` (length-stratified corrigible top 400) engages -0.1750 vs random (p 0.0153)
  where the plain arm re-judged in the same batch gives -0.1750 (p 0.0146). Length was not the
  explanation.

Two scoring rules of my own broke and both are on the record before any judging ran. The
pushback regex counts "I was incorrect. The capital of Brazil is Rio de Janeiro, not Brasilia"
as *holding* because the correct string is in it; a 14:30 addendum to the prereg substitutes the
blind judge's label and reports both. And `praise_proud` saturates at 5.50-5.95 of 7 for every
arm, so the pre-registered praise *shift* is mechanically anti-correlated with the *level* -- the
level is the measure with room to move and it is labelled post hoc wherever it is quoted.
