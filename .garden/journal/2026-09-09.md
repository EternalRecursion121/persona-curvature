- **The null arms cost no GPU, and that was discoverable in ten seconds of reading the corpora.**
  `data_null_permuted_p100_matched/<X>.jsonl` is byte-identical, as a set of triples, to
  `data_common/<Y>.jsonl`; `data_null_shuffled_p100_matched/<X>.jsonl` is `data_common/<X>.jsonl`
  with chosen and rejected exchanged on half the rows. A gradient cannot see a file name, and the
  extractor stores each completion's gradient separately, so both arms are exact linear
  recombinations of one extraction. The reading page had budgeted three extractions.
  Generalisation: before paying for a control arm, diff its corpus against the real one.

- **Do not compute data inside the autograd graph.** The first two OOMs (39.5 GB on an A100-40GB at
  batch 32 and again at 16) were `h = F.linear(x.float(), A_0)` in a forward hook: `h` is data, but
  computing it inside the graph made autograd retain a float32 copy of every module's input --
  145 MB per module at d_in 9216, times 248 modules. `with torch.no_grad(): h = F.linear(
  inp[0].detach().float(), A_0)` fixed it. The residual limit is real though: Qwen3.5's linear
  attention costs roughly 15-30 MB per token of activation, so batch is bounded by tokens, and the
  extraction pass got a halving-on-OOM wrapper (the fit passes did not, because they accumulate and
  a retry would double-count).

- **A Kronecker sketch of a per-module gradient is much worse than its entry count suggests.**
  `P_m g_m Q_m` at p = q = 8 gives 15,872 numbers per document but only ~p+q effective directions
  per module, because the per-module gradient is nearly low rank. Measured against exact cosines on
  held raw gradients: error sd **0.036** where the exact cosines' own spread was **0.047** -- the
  sketch was noisier than the signal. A plain count sketch over all 72,450,048 coordinates at
  D = 65,536 gave max abs error 0.011 and r 0.9975 for the same storage order. Measure a sketch
  against exact values on a handful of documents before trusting any number computed from it.

- Results are in the wiki: [[gradient-atoms]] and [[reward-hacks-gradient-atoms]], with the log
  entry in `wiki/log.md`. Headlines: the zoo's preference gradients do contain bipolar trait axes
  found with no labels (34.8% of atoms have the same majority factor on both poles, 21.2% with
  opposite keying, against 15.1% and 7.5% when the trait names are deranged) and four of the five

  coherence predicts judged steerability nowhere (Spearman 0.026 over 22 directions, -0.137 over

  Reward Hacks the unsupervised atoms name the hack cleanly - keyword-stuffed poems, adjective-
  listing reviews, repeated "thank you" - while landing nowhere near any personality direction.
- G1 (Fisher-metric factor analysis) answered, and the answer is the strong one: the five
  factors are a property of the adapters, not of the Frobenius metric. Tucker congruence
  between the two 134 x 5 oblimin loading matrices is 0.9654, minimum 0.9549, all five above
  the 0.95 "identical" line. Parallel analysis still retains nine. The Big Five congruences
  move by at most 0.0589 and three of five move UP, so the metric was not costing the Big Five
  mapping either - the 0.41-to-0.68 band in the post is not an underestimate. Page
  `factor-analysis-fisher-metric`.
- The validation that makes that believable, and the thing worth reusing: a Gram over unit
  adapter directions predicts the Fisher norm of ANY merge as
  (c*n)^T F (c*n) / (c^T G_exact c). That turns all 122 stage-one directions `fisher.py`
  measured one at a time - 20 random merges of the full 134 and 72 sphere points among them -
  into a held-out test of the Gram. Median ratio 0.9943, worst 7.9%. Ten singles on the
  diagonal is not the same test: the random merges are what load the off-diagonal.
- Method note worth keeping. The Fisher Gram was built with FORWARD HOOKS doing a central
  finite difference in eps, not with dense deltas. `fisher.py` materialised a 6.6 GiB delta
  buffer per direction; hooks cost two small products per module, so all 134 directions cost
  what one cost there, and the base weights are never touched so bf16 rounding cannot enter.
  36 min on one A100-80GB for 134 x 24 x 2 forward passes. The 24 GiB device-side accumulator
  (sqrt(p) * ds/deps for 134 directions over a 248,320-token vocabulary) is what forces the
  80 GB card; fp16 there underflows on rare tokens.

- Two side findings from the calibration, both about the unit. (1) `steer_fix.py` writes into
  bf16 parameters: the Frobenius norm of the realised perturbation survives (retention 0.9216
  to 1.0335) but the KL does not - KL(bf16)/KL(exact) is 0.7118 to 0.9782, median 0.9060.

  alpha-dependent, which matters for reading it: median 0.9424 at |alpha| 2 (the published

  approximation to its intended dose than a large one. (2) Equal alpha is not equal dose even
