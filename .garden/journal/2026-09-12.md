# 2026-09-12

## Is the five-factor structure an artefact of choosing Big Five marker words?

Samuel's question, three pre-registered tests, CPU only, $0.66 spent. Wiki page
[[goldberg-only-and-heldout-lexicon]], prereg `qwen35/PREREG_goldberg_only.md`,
numbers in `qwen35/analysis/goldberg_only.json`.

Answer: no, and yes, depending on which half of the question you mean.

- The **word list** is not what makes the five factors. Factoring the 100
  Goldberg markers alone returns the same five at mean Tucker 0.9923.
- The **frame generalises**. A chart built from the 100 alone places the 34
  Big-Five-agnostic Lexicon words as accurately as it places the marker words
  (mean r 0.6098 against a 0.6069 ceiling) against an LLM rater that saw no
  project data.
- The **frame is the training text**, not something weight space added. The
  chosen-minus-rejected contrast predicts held-out chart position at R^2 0.938
  against the adapters' own 0.982. sweep100's deciding test, back again on a
  different model and trait set.

The sharpest new fact: the *constitution* - the teacher's description of the
trait, the thing you would assume the geometry is a picture of - predicts almost
nothing (R^2 -0.034, Gram correlation 0.099). The *contrast* predicts almost
everything (0.938, 0.860). "The structure is in the contrast, not the text" is
now the second time this project has learned that sentence.

Two method lessons filed as notes: `loo-shortcut-degenerate-at-p-gt-n.md` (the
hat-matrix LOO picked the most overfit alpha and reported a wrong R^2) and
`tucker-denominator-inflates-when-targets-are-zero.md` (dropping rows that are
zero in the target raises congruence for free).

One wrong pre-registered sign, worth remembering: the chart's third axis is
Emotional-Stability-**positive**. "Timidity" names its negative pole.
`analyse_fa_qwen35.py:order_and_orient` orients every factor positively on its
own best Goldberg target, and `analysis/viz_fa.json#big_five_scale` carries no
reversal flag - it was right and the "reversed" in the task statement was wrong.
Any copy anywhere reading "+Timidity means more timid" is inverted.

## Addendum 2026-09-15: the 34 in isolation

Samuel asked the reverse question. Answer: the 34 alone do return all five
factors, in the right one-to-one correspondence, but as a **poorer sample** -
mean |Tucker| 0.7276, the 3rd percentile of 200 random 34-subsets of Goldberg's
own markers (median 0.8702). Pre-registered "same structure" bar was the middle
90 per cent, so it fails.

The reference distribution was the whole point of the design and it earned its
keep: without it, 0.73 from 34 variables is uninterpretable. With it, the claim
is precise - worse than about 97 per cent of equally-sized marker samples.

But a chart built from the 34 alone still places the 100 markers at **97 per cent
of the ceiling** against Goldberg's keying. L1 and L3 look contradictory and are
not: Tucker scores each rotated factor separately and an oblimin factor from 34
variables is noisy, while the five-dimensional subspace those factors span is far
better determined. Principal cosines against the 134 chart: 0.955, 0.916, 0.829,
0.773, **0.485**. Four of five dimensions cleanly, the fifth half.

And the cause is mundane and checkable: exactly **one** of the 34 words has
Arousal as its dominant chart axis, three have Imagination, against 3.1 and 5.1
expected at the markers' proportions - and Arousal and Imagination are exactly
the two factors that came back weakest (0.566, 0.549). A k-means draw over
sentence embeddings was never asked to tile the Big Five, so it did not.

Lesson worth keeping: when a statistic is computed on few variables, build the
reference distribution from the *same* number of variables drawn from the set you
trust. A bare congruence at p = 34 says nothing. And when a per-factor score and
a per-subspace score disagree, believe the subspace - rotation is the unstable
part.
